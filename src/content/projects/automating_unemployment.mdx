# Automating Unemployment

Do you know how time-consuming it is to be unemployed? I do.

Here is how I built an autonomous multi-agent workflow to reclaim **83% of the time** I was spending on the job hunt.

---

## 01 / The Breakdown

### The math ain't mathing

When I first moved to London, I was spending **12 hours a day** searching and applying for jobs.

As a career changer studying full-stack development in one of the world's most expensive cities, I faced a dilemma:

1. **Study first, apply later** — the sensible route, assuming you don't need to pay rent or buy groceries.
2. **Work and study simultaneously** — the pragmatic choice, until the math caught up with me.

In today's market, option two is nearly impossible. A single tailored application takes anywhere from **1.5 to 3 hours**. Every day demanded:

- **Sifting through hundreds of listings** to filter out scams, ghost jobs, and data miners.
- **Tailoring CVs and cover letters** to hit arbitrary 95% match thresholds on ATS scanners.
- **Managing outreach** across recruiters, hiring managers, and peers.
- **Form gymnastics:** uploading a CV and cover letter, only to re-type every line into custom portal fields just to be parsed by an LLM on the other side.

That excludes take-home assessments, cognitive tests, and multi-stage interview rounds.

Job hunting wasn't just exhausting; it was directly competing with the deep, uninterrupted focus required to master software engineering.

---

## 02 / The Strategy

### Fighting automation with automation

The tech hiring market is trapped in an **AI doom loop**:

1. Candidates face fierce competition, so they turn to AI tools to blast out hundreds of applications.
2. Companies get swamped with generic volume, so they deploy AI screens to aggressively filter candidates out.
3. Rejection rates climb, competition spikes, and the cycle accelerates.

It’s an exhausting spiral because most candidates automate the **wrong** part of the process.

Most people use AI to:

1. Generate generic CVs and cookie-cutter cover letters.
2. Hallucinate answers to application forms.

I already know I write better, more authentic applications than any generic LLM prompt. But what LLMs excel at is rapid synthesis, structuring messy data, and executing logic when paired with deterministic code.

So instead of mass-applying, I designed an AI-driven workflow using Python scripts, scheduled crons, and OpenClaw agents to automate the real operational bottlenecks:

1. **Discovery:** Automated role scraping across target job boards.
2. **Filtering:** Precision matching against tech stacks, location, and salary floors.
3. **Due Diligence:** Company research and engineering culture vibe checks.
4. **Contextual Drafting:** Pulling relevant proof points from my verified master experience.
5. **Pipeline Ops:** Centralized tracking, Slack alerts, and status monitoring.

## 04 / The Philosophy

### Human-in-the-loop: Leverage over laziness

Autonomous does not mean unaccountable. Fully automated job applications produce impersonal spam—and hiring managers can spot AI boilerplate from a mile away.

This system is built around a non-negotiable **human-in-the-loop gate**:

1. **Autonomous Prep:** Giles, Cordelia, and Willow handle scraping, filtering, and drafting in the background.
2. **Curated Slack Digest:** I receive actionable notifications with role highlights, culture signals, and drafted materials.
3. **Human Polish & Dispatch:** I review the match, verify the claims, add personal nuance, and submit the application myself.

The machines do the repetitive data extraction; I retain 100% control over voice, authenticity, and personal connection.

## 05 / The Results & Impact

### Reclaiming the engineering cycle

- **83% time reduction:** Daily job hunt overhead dropped from **12 hours down to 2**.
- **10 hours/day reclaimed:** Reinvested directly into full-stack development, algorithms, and shipping production applications.
- **Zero application burnout:** High-context applications replaced shotgun submissions, resulting in better responses and zero recruiter fatigue.
- **Compounding upside:** Freed up energy for technical networking, freelance client delivery with **Outer Office**, and building long-term software projects.
